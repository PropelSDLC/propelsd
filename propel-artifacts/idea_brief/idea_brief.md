## Description

PATH Lite is a mobile (iOS and Android) application that embeds an AI conversational assistant to capture structured, field-level patient data via voice on tablet devices used at the bedside by nurses. It exists to simplify and automate structured patient data collection and to reduce documentation time and data errors caused by time-consuming manual entry, elevated cognitive load, and navigation across multiple screens.

## Problems & Solutions

### Problem 1: Time-consuming manual data entry
**Who is affected:** Bedside nurses who currently enter patient demographic, admission, and treatment details into PATH Lite on tablet devices; patient throughput and clinic productivity are affected downstream. 
**Impact:** The BRD and interview transcript identify manual form-based entry as time-consuming, causing reduced documentation efficiency, delays in patient processing, increased risk of data errors, and lower productivity. The transcript does not provide baseline metrics (for example, average minutes per patient, error rates, or percent of incomplete records); those observability gaps require measurement during pilot or baseline data collection. The transcript specifies a primary measurable target of reducing per-patient data-entry time by 30% within the first 3-6 months. 
**How this product solves it:** A voice-driven AI conversational assistant captures nurses' spoken responses, extracts and maps structured field-level data, and guides sequential questions to complete required fields; the design targets a 30% reduction in per-patient data-entry time within the first 3-6 months.

### Problem 2: Elevated cognitive load leading to incomplete or incorrect documentation
**Who is affected:** Nurses who enter patient demographic, admission, and clinical treatment fields on PATH Lite tablets during intake and treatment workflows. 
**Impact:** The transcript links elevated cognitive load to a higher probability of incomplete documentation and an increased risk of data errors, and connects those effects to reduced documentation throughput and delays in patient processing. The BRD does not provide baseline measurements for these consequences; measure impact by tracking percent of records with one or more missing required fields, field validation failure rate, and mean time-to-complete intake. 
**How this product solves it:** Guided AI questioning presents sequential, field-level prompts so nurses address one item at a time, reducing working-memory demands and task switching; LLM cleaning extracts and normalizes conversational responses and the validation engine enforces field rules and mandatory-field checks, converting conversational answers into validated structured fields and thereby lowering the likelihood of incomplete or incorrect documentation.

### Problem 3: Workflow friction from multi-screen navigation
**Who is affected:** Nurses who use PATH Lite on tablet devices to enter patient demographic, admission, and treatment details. 
**Impact:** Navigation across multiple screens increases workflow friction, forces context switching for nurses, slows form completion, lowers documentation throughput, and contributes to delays in patient processing; the BRD links mitigation of this friction to the product's primary success metric of a 30% reduction in per-patient data-entry time. The interview transcript does not provide baseline time-per-patient or frequencies of incomplete records, so the project team must measure those operational metrics to quantify current severity and to validate improvement against the 30% target. 
**How this product solves it:** The product guides nurses through a sequential, AI-driven conversational flow that captures one structured field per question, saves partial sessions and resumes from the first unanswered question, and presents a confirmation/edit screen before submission to reduce navigation overhead and context switching.

### Problem 4: Lack of intelligent validation and guided completion
**Who is affected:** Nurses who enter patient demographic, admission, and treatment details using PATH Lite, and compliance/audit teams responsible for data completeness and accuracy. 
**Impact:** The interview transcript identifies no intelligent validation beyond basic form checks and no guided completion mechanism, which leads to skipped mandatory fields and inconsistent or unstandardized entries that produce incomplete documentation and increased data-quality exceptions. Observable metrics include percent of records with missing mandatory fields, count of audit findings related to data quality, and frequency of post-submission data-cleaning events; the transcript does not supply baseline values for these metrics. 
**How this product solves it:** The product implements a field validation engine that prevents skipping required fields and returns immediate validation feedback at the point of entry. An NLP-powered clarification and data-cleaning step extracts answers from conversational input, standardizes formats, and maps cleaned values into structured fields to improve completeness and consistency.

### Problem 5: No voice/AI assistance for structured clinical entry
**Who is affected:** Nurses who use PATH Lite on tablet devices to enter patient demographic, admission, and treatment details are directly affected by the absence of voice or AI assistance. 
**Impact:** The BRD identifies that the lack of voice/AI capability prevents automation of the speech-driven capture described in the AI Conversational Workflow and therefore prevents pursuing the BRD's measurable target to reduce data-entry time per patient by 30% within the first 3-6 months; the absence also removes the ability to track conversational-mode adoption (for example, percentage of sessions using conversational mode) and audit-log signals such as mandatory-field completion rates and audit-measured error reductions. Operational consequences named in the transcript include reduced documentation efficiency, delays in patient processing, increased risk of data errors, and lower productivity, and the BRD lacks baseline values (current minutes per patient, incomplete-record rate, error rate) required to quantify absolute and relative impact. 
**How this product solves it:** Embedding Text-to-Speech and Speech-to-Text together with an LLM that cleans and extracts answers enables nurses to speak responses that the system standardizes, validates against field rules, and maps into structured fields. Nurses activate the conversational assistant via the AI voice icon, and the assistant advances through the existing PATH Lite form sequence after each field is filled, preserving current workflow context.

## Key Features

- Conversational sequential question flow that reads structured prompts aloud, captures nurses' spoken replies via STT, and applies an LLM-based cleaning-and-extraction step to populate corresponding structured fields; acceptance: the system maps the transcript examples (numeric MRN, extracted first name, and standardized DOB) into their target fields without manual transcription. 
- Field-level validation and mandatory-field enforcement that validates extracted values against per-field rules (numeric, alphabetic, date formats), prevents skipping required fields while permitting explicit optional nulls, and triggers AI-driven clarification prompts for ambiguous or no-response cases; acceptance: a field is accepted only when it meets its format rule and unresolved responses invoke a clarification interaction. 
- Session save and resume plus confirmation/edit screen that saves partial sessions, resumes the conversational flow at the first unanswered question, and presents a review screen that permits edits prior to final submission; acceptance: a resumed session restores unanswered questions and the confirmation screen permits changes and final submission. 
- Nurse authentication with hospital and patient selection that supports secure nurse login, searchable hospital selection, display of the selected hospital's patient list, the Add New → Hemodialysis (Phase 1) entry path, and a patient-recall search that requires at least two fields including a mandatory MRN to execute; acceptance: the patient-recall search executes only when MRN plus one other field is provided and the selected hospital's patient list displays after hospital selection. 
- Audit logging and responsive UI requirements that record interaction and audit events and render correctly on tablet sizes named in the transcript (7-inch to 12.9-inch) in both portrait and landscape using the primary theme color #1566A7 and minimum 44x44pt touch targets with a clear clinical visual hierarchy; acceptance: interaction events appear in audit logs and UI verification tests confirm correct rendering, theme application, and touch target sizing; note: the BRD does not name implementation owners, a measurement plan, or a pilot timeline, and reliable spoken-entry accuracy plus AI extraction tuning require additional data collection, annotation, model monitoring, and an operational plan-expect an achievable MVP in 3-6 months but validated success only after a 6-12+ month pilot with defined owners and measurement criteria.

## Success Criteria

- Reduce data-entry time per patient by 30% within the first 3-6 months after deployment; the transcript defines this primary target but omits a measurement method, pilot plan, and an owner for validation, and a feasibility review indicates reliable validation likely requires a 6-12+ month pilot with iterative ML tuning, data collection, and operational readiness activities before the 30% reduction can be confirmed. 
- Increase the mandatory-field completion rate as recorded in audit logs by enforcing mandatory-field rules; the transcript specifies mandatory-field enforcement and audit logging but does not define a numeric completion threshold or an owner, so the project must set a measurable target, audit cadence, and an owner during the pilot. 
- Reduce the incidence of incomplete documentation and data errors as measured by periodic documentation audits; the transcript identifies these problems but does not state a quantitative reduction goal, so acceptance criteria must include a target percentage reduction, audit sampling method, audit frequency, and an owner prior to validation. 
- Measure adoption of conversational (voice) mode by tracking the percentage of patient-entry sessions using conversational mode via session logs; the transcript requires conversational mode availability but does not specify a target adoption rate or pilot benchmarks, so the pilot must define adoption targets, measurement cadence, and an owner to evaluate success.

## Scope

**In:** The scope includes a mobile application on iOS and Android. The scope includes nurse authentication for access control. The scope includes hospital selection as a precondition to patient workflows. The scope includes the existing patient list for the selected hospital. The scope includes the "Add New → Hemodialysis" workflow limited to Phase 1. The scope includes the Patient Recall Search with the BRD rule that Medical Record Number (MRN) is mandatory and a minimum of two fields (including MRN) are required to execute a search. The scope includes AI voice-based patient details entry driven by conversational question flow, speech-to-text, and LLM cleaning/extraction as described in the transcript. The scope includes a field validation engine that enforces mandatory-field rules. The scope includes a confirmation and edit screen presented before submission. The scope includes session save and resume capability that resumes from the first unanswered question. The scope includes audit logging of session activity and submission events.

**Out:** The scope excludes production EHR integration; the transcript lists this item as out of scope but does not record a rationale or a named owner, creating ambiguity about post-submission handoff and data-source boundaries. The scope excludes a full PATH Lite replacement; the transcript lists this item as out of scope while also assuming integration into existing PATH Lite workflows (see Assumptions and Questions), and the transcript does not name an owner to validate or manage that dependency. The scope excludes integration with live hospital systems; the transcript lists this item as out of scope but does not provide a rationale or an identified owner, leaving the source of the "existing patient list" unspecified. The scope excludes non-Hemodialysis treatments in Phase 1 because the transcript explicitly defines Phase 1 as focused on Hemodialysis only. The Patient Recall Search MRN + additional-field rule is included in scope per the BRD but the transcript does not validate the rule operationally nor assign an owner to maintain or approve it, which represents a scope risk. The transcript defines a primary success target to reduce data-entry time per patient by 30% (see Measurable Outcomes) but does not provide a measurement method, pilot plan, or an owner to validate that metric; this absence is recorded here as an unresolved scope boundary.
